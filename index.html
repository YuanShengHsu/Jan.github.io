About

I'm a physician-scientist in Taichung, Taiwan, working in bone and mineral health and clinical pharmacoepidemiology. I trained as an MD-PhD at China Medical University; my doctoral research, in the Wen-Hwa Lee lab at Academia Sinica, examined how metabolism drives genome instability.

My current program builds and validates polygenic risk scores for osteoporotic fracture in a Taiwanese DXA-linked biobank, and runs real-world evidence studies of skeletal disease across federated networks — with the causal-inference methods (target trial emulation, propensity scores) that observational data demand. It's mentored by Dr. Kun-Huei Chen, Chair of Orthopedic Surgery at Taichung Veterans General Hospital.

The through-line is moving cleanly between scales — mechanism, translation, and population evidence — so a finding means the same thing at the bench as it does in the clinic.

Polygenic scores for 5-year osteoporotic fracture risk

Benchmarked 66 published polygenic scores for bone density, then tested whether the strongest one adds real predictive value for major osteoporotic fracture in peri-menopausal Han Chinese women — beyond the clinical factors already in use — using Fine-Gray competing-risk models and BMD-stratified analysis.
